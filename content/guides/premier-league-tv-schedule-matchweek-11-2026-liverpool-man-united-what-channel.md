## Why Premier League games are confusing to find on US TV

The Premier League doesn't use local channels or regional sports networks in the US. NBC Sports owns all the rights and splits each weekend three ways:

- **USA Network** gets most of the big games. It's a cable channel, not a free broadcast channel, so an antenna won't pick it up.
- **NBC** (the free broadcast network) takes a game on some weekends, usually a Saturday or Sunday marquee match. There's no NBC game in Matchweek 11.
- **Peacock** streams every game that isn't on NBC or USA Network, which usually means the Saturday 10 a.m. ET window. Those games are **only** on Peacock, so a cable or live TV package alone won't get them.

There are no blackouts. You see the same games in every US city.

## How to watch the Premier League without cable

All of these are legal, licensed options:

- **[Peacock](/go/peacock/):** the home of every game that isn't on TV. It's the only place to watch the Peacock-only games, and it also streams NBC's Premier League games. USA Network games need a TV package that carries USA Network.
- **[YouTube TV](/go/youtube-tv/), [Fubo](/go/fubo/), [Hulu + Live TV](/go/hulu-live/) and [DIRECTV Stream](/go/directv-stream/):** carry USA Network and local NBC in most markets, covering every game that isn't Peacock-only.
- **[Sling TV](/go/sling/):** Sling Blue has USA Network, and local NBC only in select markets.
- **[An antenna](/go/antenna/):** free, but only for the occasional game on NBC. It won't get USA Network or Peacock games.

USA Network carries most of the big games, so for every match you need both: Peacock plus a service that carries USA Network. Lineups vary by ZIP code, so check a service's channel list before you pay.
